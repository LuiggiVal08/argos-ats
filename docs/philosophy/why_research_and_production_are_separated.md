# Why Research and Production Are Separated

Research operates in Jupyter notebooks and Python scripts with pandas, matplotlib, and seaborn — optimized for iterative exploration, visualization, and rapid hypothesis testing. Production runs on NestJS and FastAPI with strict hexagonal architecture, contract validation, and real-time event streams. These are fundamentally different environments with different dependency profiles, performance requirements, and reliability guarantees. Running research code in production would introduce dependency bloat, accidental experimental code paths, and version conflicts.

The boundary is intentionally one-way: research imports production modules (LabelEngine, FeatureEngine) to ensure the research pipeline exactly matches the production pipeline. But production never imports from research. This prevents a class of subtle bugs where an experimental transformation accidentally becomes part of the live pipeline. The key validation phases (35, 375, 38, 39, 4) are standalone scripts that consume production modules to reproduce the pipeline exactly, but they remain in the research domain — they are not on the critical path of the live trading loop. This separation ensures that exploration and production can evolve at their own pace without compromising system integrity.
